# NYC Taxi Operations Intelligence: Business Guide

## 1. Executive Summary

NYC Taxi Operations Intelligence turns NYC Taxi and Limousine Commission trip records into trusted operational information for fleet managers, finance teams, compliance officers, and executives.

The platform answers four recurring questions:

1. Where is demand occurring, and where should vehicles be redirected?
2. How are revenue, tips, and trip economics changing by borough?
3. Which trips or zones require quality, compliance, or financial investigation?
4. Can service coverage be demonstrated consistently across neighborhoods?

The project is a migration of the YG-Pipeline from an on-premises ClickHouse, Spark, Docker, and Terraform stack to Databricks. Databricks now provides the ingestion, Delta Lake storage, Spark processing, SQL analytics, job orchestration, Unity Catalog governance, and dashboard delivery.

## 2. Business Context

Taxi operators serve more than 260 zones with a fleet whose demand changes by hour, borough, weekday, weather, events, airport activity, and traffic conditions. Raw trip files are useful for record keeping but are too detailed and inconsistent for direct operational decisions.

The platform converts those records into a governed chain of evidence:

```text
Source files
  -> Bronze source records
  -> Silver trusted trips
  -> Gold business metrics
  -> Client dashboards and investigations
```

This design separates operational reporting from raw-data complexity. A dashboard user can ask a business question without knowing how files were downloaded, how duplicates were identified, or how invalid numerical values were quarantined.

## 3. Stakeholders and Business Questions

### Fleet Managers and Dispatchers

**Questions:**

- Which boroughs and hours have the highest trip demand?
- Which zones appear underserved today?
- Which areas are becoming saturated over the last two weeks?
- Where could vehicles be redirected to reduce empty cruising?

**Answering products:**

- Fleet Operations dashboard
- `nyc_taxi.gold.trip_summary`
- Zone and date dimensions
- Hourly demand and saturation datasets

**Important interpretation:**

Trip volume is a demand signal, not a direct count of waiting customers or available vehicles. Dispatch decisions should combine these metrics with fleet availability and real-time vehicle location data when those sources are connected.

### Finance Analysts and Executives

**Questions:**

- How much revenue was generated by borough and day?
- Is revenue per trip increasing or declining?
- How are tip percentages changing?
- Which fare anomalies represent material revenue exposure?

**Answering products:**

- Financial Performance dashboard
- `nyc_taxi.gold.daily_borough_metrics`
- Fare and anomaly fields in `nyc_taxi.gold.trip_summary`
- `nyc_taxi.quarantine.taxi_reversed_latest`
- `nyc_taxi.quarantine.taxi_numerical_outliers_latest`

**Important interpretation:**

Anomaly flags identify records for investigation. They do not prove fraud, meter tampering, or intentional misconduct. Finance and compliance teams should review the original quarantined record and supporting operational evidence.

### Compliance Officers and Regulators

**Questions:**

- Which zones are underserved during the reporting period?
- How many zones have service, and how does that differ by borough?
- Are service gaps worse on weekends or weekdays?
- How many anomalous, reversed, duplicate, or invalid records were detected?
- Can the organization reproduce the data and logic behind a reported metric?

**Answering products:**

- Compliance and Quality dashboard
- `nyc_taxi.gold.dim_zone`
- `nyc_taxi.gold.dim_date`
- `nyc_taxi.quarantine` tables
- Quality-check notebook output

**Important interpretation:**

The current coverage status is based on trip-count thresholds. It is an analytical proxy for observed service, not a complete regulatory determination until vehicle availability, wait times, operating hours, and official TLC rules are added.

### Operations Leads

**Questions:**

- Are trip durations increasing by borough?
- Are distance and duration distributions behaving normally?
- Are extreme fares, distances, or durations concentrated in one area or period?
- Is data quality stable after each ingestion run?

**Answering products:**

- Gold trip summary
- Daily borough metrics
- Quality-check notebook
- Numerical-outlier and duplicate quarantine tables

## 4. Business Data Products

### Trip Summary

`nyc_taxi.gold.trip_summary` is the main analytical contract. It combines cleaned Silver trips with date and zone attributes, including borough, neighborhood, time of day, trip duration, distance, revenue, tips, payment labels, rate labels, and anomaly status.

Use it when the question requires trip-level detail or custom aggregation.

### Daily Borough Performance

`nyc_taxi.gold.daily_borough_metrics` provides daily KPIs by pickup borough:

- Total trips
- Fare, tip, and total revenue
- Average tip percentage
- Average duration and distance
- Zones with pickups
- Revenue per trip
- Anomaly count

Use it for executive and finance reporting where fast, stable aggregations matter more than individual trip detail.

### Zone Dimension

`nyc_taxi.gold.dim_zone` translates TLC location IDs into borough and zone names and adds region and zone-type classifications.

Use it to make operational reporting understandable to business users. Reports should show `Manhattan — Midtown`, not only `LocationID = 161`.

### Date Dimension

`nyc_taxi.gold.dim_date` provides calendar attributes for 2020–2026, including month, quarter, weekday, weekend, and holiday flags.

Use it to keep time filtering and period comparisons consistent across dashboards.

### Quarantine Products

Quarantine is part of the business product, not a hidden failure folder. It preserves evidence for investigation:

- Duplicate trip business keys
- Zero-distance trips
- Reversed or negative monetary records
- Numerical outliers with explicit reasons

## 6. Data Quality and Trust Model

The platform uses four trust stages:

1. **Preserve:** Bronze retains source-oriented records.
2. **Detect:** notebooks identify duplicates, reversals, invalid timestamps, and numerical outliers.
3. **Quarantine:** suspicious records are saved with context and reasons.
4. **Publish:** Silver and Gold contain the analytical contract used by dashboards.

### Numerical outlier policy

The current policy quarantines:

- Trip distance greater than 100 miles
- Trip duration greater than 1,440 minutes
- Fare amount greater than 500 dollars
- Total amount greater than 500 dollars
- Tip amount greater than 200 dollars
- Toll amount greater than 200 dollars
- Negative fare or total amounts
- Invalid passenger-count conditions

These thresholds are operational data-quality rules, not universal definitions of fraud or invalidity. They should be reviewed as more months of data and business baselines become available.

### Inferred values

The Silver contract records whether the pipeline inferred values for passenger count, payment type, or rate code. Invalid rate codes map to `0` (`Unknown`) instead of being silently labeled as standard rate.

### Duplicate identity

The current business key is derived from pickup time, dropoff time, pickup location, and dropoff location. All rows sharing that key are quarantined before Silver publication to prevent ambiguous Delta merges.

## 7. Data Flow and Operating Workflow

### Load a targeted period

Use `download_files` with a two-digit month and year. The job checks the Volume first and avoids downloading a file that already exists.

### Load the historical range

Use `backfill_download` to submit 84 month-year combinations from 2020 through 2026.

### Run analytics

After files arrive, `etl_job` runs:

1. Schema and Volume setup
2. Auto Loader ingestion
3. Bronze ingestion validation
4. Duplicate quarantine
5. Zero-distance quarantine
6. Zone lookup augmentation
7. Silver transformation and outlier quarantine
8. Duplicate cleanup
9. Quality checks
10. Gold date and zone dimensions
11. Gold trip summary
12. Daily borough metrics

## 9. Limitations

- The current source represents observed trips, not all demand or all available vehicles.
- Current coverage thresholds are configurable business heuristics.
- Anomaly flags support triage and do not establish fraud.
- The pipeline currently focuses on NYC Green Taxi records and TLC zone lookup data.
- Production access controls, retention, and regulator-facing evidence workflows require additional implementation.
